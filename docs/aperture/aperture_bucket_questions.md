## Frameworks Reference

The questions across all 13 buckets are built on the following proven frameworks, adapted for small business owners — specifically Persian immigrant entrepreneurs in the US and Canada. The AI should understand these frameworks as the reasoning behind why certain questions are asked, not as scripts to follow.

| Framework | Author | Primary Buckets Used In |
|---|---|---|
| Business Model Canvas | Alexander Osterwalder | 3, 4, 7, 10, 11 |
| $100M Offers / $100M Leads | Alex Hormozi | 3, 4, 5, 6, 11 |
| SPIN Selling | Neil Rackham | 5 |
| StoryBrand | Donald Miller | 4, 5, 6 |
| Profit First | Mike Michalowicz | 7 |
| Scaling Up | Verne Harnish | 7, 8, 9, 10 |
| EOS / Traction | Gino Wickman | 8, 9, 13 |
| E-Myth | Michael Gerber | 9, 12 |
| Jobs-to-be-Done | Clayton Christensen | 3, 4, 11 |
| Blue Ocean Strategy | Kim & Mauborgne | 11 |
| Start With Why | Simon Sinek | 13 |
| Good to Great / Hedgehog Concept | Jim Collins | 13 |
| Five Dysfunctions of a Team | Patrick Lencioni | 8 |
| Who | Geoff Smart | 8 |
| Jab Jab Jab Right Hook | Gary Vaynerchuk | 6 |
| Permission Marketing | Seth Godin | 6 |
| Simple Numbers | Greg Crabtree | 7 |

**Coaching experience** (Ali Davoodifar) is layered throughout all buckets — especially on immigrant context, language confidence, community involvement, personal brand, other responsibilities, strengths and weaknesses, and the principle that the AI must always have enough data to answer what the owner will actually ask.

**Key design principle:** Questions are targets for the AI, not a script. The AI reads what it needs to find out and draws it out through natural conversation — asking follow-ups, going deeper on what's interesting, skipping what's already known. The complexity is on the AI side. The owner just talks.

**Calculated metrics principle:** Where the AI has enough data from other buckets, it calculates key metrics automatically — P&L estimate, customer lifetime value, acquisition cost, retention rate, break-even — rather than asking for them directly. All calculated figures are saved as estimates and refined over time.

## Bucket 2 — Your Story

**Everyone:**

1. Before this business, what did you do for work — here and back home if applicable?
2. Why this specific business? Out of everything you could have done, what made you choose this?
3. What are you genuinely good at — not what you think you should be good at, but what comes naturally to you? What do people consistently compliment you on?
4. Outside the business, what are your main responsibilities right now — family, another job, financial obligations, caregiving?
5. How do you spend your weekends and free time?
6. Who are you doing this for — and what does winning actually look like for you personally, not just the business?
7. What communities are you part of and how active are you in them — professional groups, local networks, religious communities, cultural organizations, neighborhood associations?

8. How do you want to be known — if someone described you to a potential customer, what would you want them to say?
9. What are you not good at — what drains you, what do you avoid, what do you always end up doing badly?

**If immigrant (ask first: "Were you born outside the US/Canada?"):**

10. How long have you been here, and where do you feel you are in that journey?
11. How comfortable are you using English in professional situations — selling, networking, meetings?
12. What did you leave behind professionally when you moved — credentials, reputation, experience, a network?

## Bucket 3 — Customers & Market

**Layer 1 — Current customers (basics)**

1. Who is actually buying from you right now — describe a real customer, not a category?
2. Are they individuals or businesses?
3. Where are they — local, specific neighborhood, online, specific community?
4. Roughly how old are they, male/female/mix?
5. How many active customers do you have right now?
6. How often do they buy — one time or repeat?
7. How do they currently find you?

**Layer 2 — Target market (basics)**

8. Who is your ideal customer — describe them specifically?
9. Are you targeting individuals or businesses?
10. Is your target market local, national, online, or a specific community?
11. Do you serve a specific cultural or ethnic community, a general market, or both?
12. What is their demographic — age, gender, income level, lifestyle?
13. What do they care about most?
14. How large is your target market — niche or broad?
15. Where does your target market spend time — in person and online?
16. What are their hobbies and interests?

**Layer 3 — The gap**

17. Is who you're targeting and who's actually buying the same person — or different?

**Layer 4 — Deep customer understanding**

18. What does the customer actually want — not the product, the outcome?
19. What is their external problem — the surface one they'd describe to you?
20. What is their internal problem — how does it make them feel?
21. What were they doing before they found you — what alternative were they using?
22. What would make them leave you and go somewhere else?
23. What job is the customer actually hiring your business to do?
24. Are they in enough pain to pay — or is this a nice-to-have for them?

**Layer 5 — Past-focused questions**

25. Who was your most loyal customer ever — describe them specifically. How did they find you, what were they like, what kept them coming back?
26. Who was your best customer ever — what made them the best?
27. Who is a customer you wish you had more of — and why?

**Layer 6 — Customer feedback**

28. What do customers actually say about you — in reviews, in person, word of mouth?
29. What exact words do they use to describe your business or product?
30. What do they complain about — even the ones who stay?
31. Have you ever lost a good customer — what happened?

**Layer 7 — Value metrics (AI derives if data exists)**

32. How long does a typical customer stay with you — months, years, one time only?
33. How much does a typical customer spend with you over their entire relationship with the business?
34. Do you know what it costs you to acquire one new customer — in time, money, or effort?

*If data from other buckets is sufficient, the AI calculates customer lifetime value and customer acquisition cost automatically and saves to memory pool.*

## Bucket 4 — Products & Services

**Layer 1 — Inventory**

1. Walk me through everything you sell — not just the main thing, everything?
2. For each one — what exactly is it, what's included, and what does it cost?
3. Which ones are you most known for?

**Layer 2 — Structure**

4. Which is your core offer — the thing that drives most of your revenue?
5. What's secondary — things that support or complement the main offer?
6. Do you have anything you offer after someone buys — upsells, cross-sells, add-ons?
7. Do you have any bundles or packages?
8. Is anything recurring or subscription-based, or is everything one-time?

**Layer 3 — Delivery**

9. How do you deliver what you sell — in person, online, at their location, shipped?
10. How long does it take from purchase to the customer getting the result?
11. What does the customer experience from the moment they buy to the moment it's done?

**Layer 4 — Value & Outcome**

12. What does the customer actually get — not the product, the result?
13. How likely does the customer feel they are to get that result with you?
14. How much effort does the customer have to put in to get the result?
15. Who is the customer before they buy from you — and who are they after?

**Layer 5 — Clarity & Differentiation**

16. Can you explain what you sell in one sentence a stranger would immediately understand?
17. Why would someone choose you over every other option — including doing nothing?
18. What makes what you offer impossible to directly compare to a competitor?

**Layer 6 — Gaps & History**

19. What have you tried selling that didn't work — and why do you think it failed?
20. Is there something you could sell but aren't — what's stopping you?
21. What do customers ask for that you don't currently offer?

**Layer 7 — Performance per product**

22. Which product or service takes the most of your time relative to what it earns?
23. Do you get refunds or complaints more on one product than others — which one and why?
24. If you could only sell one thing, which would it be and why?

## Bucket 5 — Sales & Conversion

**Layer 1 — The Journey**

1. Walk me through every step from the moment someone first contacts you to the moment they pay — what actually happens?
2. How long does that process take on average?
3. Does most of your new business come to you — or do you go out and find it?

**Layer 2 — Introduction**

4. How do you introduce yourself to a potential customer?
5. What do you say when someone asks "what do you do?"
6. How do you explain your offer — what words do you use?
7. Does your introduction change depending on who you're talking to?

**Layer 3 — The Sales Conversation**

8. What questions do you ask a potential customer before you present your offer?
9. How do you find out if they have a real problem worth solving?
10. Do you explore what it's costing them to not solve this problem?
11. Who does most of the talking in a sales conversation — you or them?
12. When exactly do you present your offer and price?

**Layer 4 — Channels & Materials**

13. Where does the sales conversation actually happen — WhatsApp, phone call, in person, DM, email?
14. Do you use different channels for different types of customers?
15. What do you send potential customers — a price list, a brochure, a link, a voice message?
16. What does your proposal or offer look like when you present it?

**Layer 5 — Scripts & Messages**

17. What do you say in your first message or first call to a potential customer — walk me through it?
18. Do you have a script or template — or do you wing it every time?
19. What does your follow-up message look like — what exactly do you say?
20. How many follow-up messages do you send and how far apart?

**Layer 6 — The Close**

21. What exactly do you ask the customer to do at the end of a first interaction?
22. Do you have a guarantee — what happens if the customer isn't happy?
23. Is your close consistent every time or does it change?

**Layer 7 — Conversion & Lost Leads**

24. Out of 10 people who show serious interest, how many actually buy?
25. What happens to the ones who don't buy — do they disappear or do you follow up?
26. What's the most common reason people don't buy?

**Layer 8 — System**

27. Do you follow the same process every time you sell — or does it change?
28. Is your sales process written down anywhere — or is it all in your head?
29. If you were unavailable for a week, could someone else handle a sales conversation the same way you do?

**Layer 9 — Critical conversion factors**

30. What objections do you hear most often — price, timing, trust, need to think about it?
31. How do you handle each of those objections — what do you say?
32. How quickly do you respond when a new inquiry comes in — minutes, hours, days?

## Bucket 6 — Marketing & Visibility

**Layer 1 — Inventory**

1. What marketing channels are you currently active on — website, Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, YouTube, email, Google Business, other?
2. What's the URL or handle for each one?
3. Are you running any paid ads right now — Meta, Google, TikTok, other?

**Layer 2 — Numbers & Results**

4. How many followers do you have on each platform?
5. How many people are on your email list?
6. Do you know how many people visit your website per month?
7. What is your Google Business rating and how many reviews do you have?
8. How much are you spending on ads per month?
9. What results are those ads producing — leads, sales, cost per lead?
10. Which channel is bringing in the most actual paying customers right now?
11. Which channel takes the most of your time but produces the least results?

**Layer 3 — Activity & Detail on Main Channels**

12. How often are you posting on your main platform?
13. What kind of content do you post — video, photo, stories, reels, written?
14. Who creates your content — you, someone else, a mix?
15. Do you have a content strategy or do you post when you remember?
16. How do you respond to comments and DMs — how fast, what do you say?
17. What is your best performing post ever — what was it about, why do you think it worked?
18. For ads — what are you running, what's the offer, who is the audience you're targeting?
19. What was your best performing ad ever — the copy, the image, the audience, the result?
20. For email — how often do you send, what do you send?
21. For Google Business — are you actively managing it, responding to reviews?

**Layer 4 — Message Clarity**

22. Can a stranger understand what you do and who it's for within 5 seconds of landing on your page?
23. When someone sees your content, what is the one thing you want them to do next — is that clear?
24. Do you have a direct call to action — book now, buy now, call us?
25. Do you have a transitional call to action — something free or low commitment to attract people who aren't ready to buy yet?
26. Are you talking about yourself and your business in your content — or about your customer and their problem?

**Layer 5 — Audience & Lead Generation**

27. Is your target audience actually active on the platforms you're using?
28. Do you have anything you offer for free to attract potential customers — a lead magnet, a free consultation, a sample?
29. Are you building an owned audience — email list, community — or are you purely relying on social media followers?
30. Do you know what it costs you to acquire one new customer?

**Layer 6 — Content Strategy**

31. Out of every 10 pieces of content you post, how many are pure value and how many are promotional?
32. Do you create different content for different platforms or post the same thing everywhere?
33. What is your hook — why would someone stop scrolling and pay attention to your content?
34. What have you tried in marketing that didn't work — and why do you think it failed?
35. What's working right now that you should be doing more of?
36. What are your competitors doing in their marketing that seems to be working?

**Layer 7 — Reputation & Word of Mouth**

37. How much of your new business comes from referrals vs. active marketing?
38. Do you actively ask satisfied customers to refer others — how?
39. Do you ask customers for reviews — when and how?
40. How do you respond to negative reviews or complaints publicly?

**Layer 8 — Personal Brand**

41. Do you appear personally in your content — on camera, voice, writing?
42. If not — what's stopping you?
43. Do people follow you personally or just the business account?
44. Are you known by name in your market — or is the business the brand?

**Layer 9 — Community Marketing**

45. Are you marketing in a specific language — English, Farsi, both, other?
46. Do you show up in community spaces relevant to your audience — events, directories, WhatsApp groups, Telegram channels, local associations?
47. Have you been featured in any community media, podcasts, or platforms relevant to your audience?

## Bucket 7 — Money & Finance

**Layer 1 — Revenue**

1. What is your monthly revenue right now — roughly?
2. Is that number consistent month to month or does it fluctuate significantly?
3. How many customers do you serve per month on average?
4. What does a typical customer spend per transaction?
5. Do you have more than one revenue stream — what are they?
6. Which revenue stream brings in the most money?
7. Which revenue stream is the most profitable — not the biggest, the most profitable?

**Layer 2 — Profit & Margins**

8. Do you know your actual profit after all expenses at the end of each month?
9. Do you know your gross margin — what's left after the direct cost of delivering your product or service?
10. Do you know your net margin — what's left after every expense including your own pay?
11. Which product or service has the highest margin?
12. Which has the lowest — and why do you still sell it?
13. What does it cost you to deliver one unit of your main product or service?

**Layer 3 — Expenses**

14. What are your three biggest monthly expenses?
15. Which of your expenses are fixed — the same every month regardless of sales?
16. Which are variable — they change based on how much you sell?
17. What percentage of your revenue goes to labor — employees, contractors, yourself?
18. Are there expenses you're paying for that you're not sure are worth it?

**Layer 4 — Owner Pay**

19. Do you pay yourself a regular salary — how much?
20. Or do you take whatever is left after expenses?
21. If you hired someone to replace yourself in the business, what would you have to pay them?
22. Is the business actually profitable once you pay yourself a fair market salary?

**Layer 5 — Cash Flow**

23. Do you ever run short on cash before revenue comes in?
24. How long between when you spend money and when you get it back — your cash conversion cycle?
25. Do you have slow months — when, and how do you handle them?
26. Are you growing revenue but feeling like there's never enough cash?

**Layer 6 — Reserves & Stability**

27. Do you have cash reserves set aside for slow months — how much?
28. Do you set aside money for taxes every month — do you have a tax account?
29. What would happen to the business if revenue stopped for 60 days?

**Layer 7 — Debt & Credit**

30. Does the business carry any debt right now — loans, credit cards, lines of credit?
31. What are the terms — interest rate, monthly payment, how long remaining?
32. Are you personally guaranteeing any business debt?
33. Have you ever applied for business financing and been rejected?
34. Do you use business credit cards — what's the current balance?

**Layer 8 — Pricing**

35. How did you set your prices originally?
36. When did you last raise your prices?
37. Are your prices based on the value you deliver — or on what competitors charge?
38. What would happen to demand if you raised your prices by 20% tomorrow?
39. Do you ever discount — when and why?
40. Do different customers pay different prices for the same thing?

**Layer 9 — Financial Systems**

41. Do you have a separate business bank account?
42. Do you use any accounting software — QuickBooks, Wave, FreshBooks, other?
43. Do you work with an accountant or bookkeeper?
44. Do you separate personal and business finances completely?
45. Do you have a monthly budget — planned expenses vs actual?
46. Do you do any financial forecasting — projecting revenue and expenses forward?

**Layer 10 — Financial Awareness**

47. How often do you look at your numbers?
48. Do you have a P&L — profit and loss statement — and do you understand it?
49. Do you know your break-even point — the minimum revenue needed to cover all costs?
50. What financial number do you watch most closely — and why that one?
51. What number do you wish you knew but don't?

**Layer 11 — Calculated Metrics (AI derives from available data)**

*The AI calculates these from answers already given — it does not ask for them directly. If data is insufficient, it asks the minimum questions needed to estimate.*

- Monthly P&L estimate
- Gross and net margin percentages
- Customer acquisition cost
- Customer lifetime value
- Retention rate
- Break-even point

*All calculated figures are saved to memory as estimates with a note that they are derived, not verified — and refined as more data becomes available.*

## Bucket 8 — Team & People

**Layer 1 — Inventory**

1. Do you work alone or do you have people helping you?
2. How many people work in the business — full time, part time, contractors?
3. What does each person do — walk me through every role?
4. Are they employees or contractors?
5. How long has each person been with you?
6. What do you pay them — salary, hourly, commission, per project?
7. How does that compare to what the market pays for the same role?

**Layer 2 — Right People Right Seats**

8. If you had to rehire everyone tomorrow — would you hire all of them again?
9. Is there anyone in the wrong role — good person, just not the right fit for what they're doing?
10. Is there anyone who simply isn't the right person for the business at all?
11. Does everyone on your team share the values you want the business to stand for?
12. Do you have A players — people who would be hard to replace — or mostly B players?

**Layer 3 — Accountability & Clarity**

13. Does each person know exactly what they're responsible for?
14. Is there anything that falls through the cracks — nobody's clearly responsible for it?
15. Is anything documented — job descriptions, processes, expectations — or is it all informal?
16. Is there anyone doing too many things at once, including you?

**Layer 4 — Dependency & Risk**

17. What would happen if your best person left tomorrow?
18. Is there anyone the business cannot function without — other than you?
19. Are people following systems — or following you personally?
20. Could someone new learn a role from documentation — or only by watching someone do it?

**Layer 5 — Hiring Process**

21. When you've hired in the past, how did you decide who to hire?
22. Do you have a hiring process — or does it happen differently every time?
23. What questions do you ask in an interview?
24. Do you check references seriously — or as a formality?
25. What has gone wrong with hires in the past — and why do you think it happened?
26. What qualities do you look for that you've learned matter most?

**Layer 6 — Where to Hire**

27. Where do you currently look when you need someone — Indeed, referrals, community, social media, other?
28. What channels have worked best for finding good people?
29. Have you hired from within your cultural or ethnic community — how did that go?
30. Have you used staffing agencies or recruiters — what was the experience?

**Layer 7 — What to Hire**

31. What is the next role you need to fill — what would free you up the most?
32. What are you doing yourself right now that someone else should be doing?
33. What is the biggest time drain in your week that a hire could solve?
34. Do you have an org chart — even a rough one in your head?
35. What does your organizational structure look like today?
36. What would you need it to look like in 2 years to support where you want to go?

**Layer 8 — Managing People**

37. How do you communicate with your team day to day — meetings, WhatsApp, check-ins, ad hoc?
38. How do you set expectations — do people know exactly what good looks like?
39. How do you handle it when someone isn't performing?
40. Do you give regular feedback — or only when something goes wrong?
41. What's your biggest frustration with managing people right now?
42. Have you ever had to let someone go — what happened, how did you handle it?

**Layer 9 — Culture & Trust**

43. Do people on your team speak up when something is wrong — or stay quiet?
44. Is there trust between people in the business?
45. Do people come to you with every decision — or do they take initiative?
46. What kind of culture do you want the business to have — and how close is it to that today?

## Bucket 9 — Operations

**Layer 1 — Daily Reality**

1. Walk me through a typical day in your business — what actually happens from start to finish?
2. What are the core activities that have to happen every single day for the business to function?
3. Who does what — which tasks belong to you vs your team?
4. How do you start and end the day — what opens and closes the operation?

**Layer 2 — Weekly & Monthly Rhythms**

5. What happens every week without fail — recurring tasks, meetings, check-ins?
6. What happens monthly — reporting, reviews, restocking, billing?
7. Do you have any regular meeting rhythm with your team — daily huddle, weekly check-in?
8. How do you track whether things are on track week to week?

**Layer 3 — Infrastructure**

9. What physical space does the business depend on — office, store, kitchen, warehouse?
10. What equipment is essential — what would stop the business if it broke down?
11. What software or platforms does the operation run on day to day?
12. What would be the first thing to fail if your internet or main tool went down?

**Layer 4 — Core Processes**

13. What are the 3 to 7 core processes that run your business — the things that happen the same way every time?
14. How long does each core process take from start to finish?
15. Are those processes written down anywhere — or do they live in your head?
16. If you hired someone new tomorrow, how would they learn how things are done here?
17. Are people doing things the same way every time — or does it change depending on who's doing it?

**Layer 5 — Systems vs Memory**

18. Is the business running on documented systems — or on your personal knowledge and memory?
19. If you disappeared for 30 days, what would stop working first?
20. Could someone run this business from documentation alone — or only by watching you?
21. What process, if documented properly, would make the biggest difference right now?

**Layer 6 — Owner Dependency**

22. How much of the business depends on you personally being present?
23. Are you spending most of your time doing the work — or designing and improving how the work gets done?
24. What would you need to change to step back from the day-to-day without things falling apart?
25. Have you ever tried to take time off — what happened to the business while you were gone?

**Layer 7 — Bottlenecks**

26. Where does the business slow down or back up most consistently?
27. What is the one constraint that, if removed, would allow the business to move faster?
28. Where do customers experience delays or frustration in your process?
29. Is there a step in your operation that always creates problems — what is it?

**Layer 8 — Waste & Low-Value Activity**

30. What takes a lot of your time but creates very little value?
31. What are you doing manually that could be automated?
32. What activities could be eliminated entirely without the customer noticing?
33. Where is money being spent in operations that isn't producing a return?

**Layer 9 — Key Activities & Value Creation**

34. What is the one activity in your business that creates the most value — are you protecting time for it?
35. What should you be spending more time on but keep getting pulled away from?
36. Which activities are essential to your value proposition — and which could be outsourced?

**Layer 10 — Breaking Down & Root Causes**

37. What breaks down most often in your operation?
38. Have you fixed the root cause — or do you keep fixing the same symptom?
39. What is the most expensive mistake your operation makes repeatedly — what does it cost you in time or money?
40. What do customers complain about that is actually an operational problem?

## Bucket 10 — Partners & Dependencies

**Layer 1 — Suppliers**

1. Who are your main suppliers — what do you buy from them and how often?
2. How critical is each supplier — what would happen if they disappeared tomorrow?
3. Do you have backup suppliers for your most critical inputs?
4. How do you manage supplier relationships — is it transactional or do you invest in the relationship?
5. Have you ever had a supplier fail you — what happened and how did you handle it?

**Layer 2 — Referral Sources**

6. Who sends you customers regularly — individuals, businesses, communities?
7. How did those referral relationships start?
8. Is there a formal arrangement — do you pay for referrals, trade referrals, or is it informal?
9. How reliable are these referral sources — consistent or occasional?
10. What percentage of your new business comes from referrals?
11. What do you offer in return for referrals — money, reciprocal referrals, gifts, nothing?

**Layer 3 — Platform Dependencies**

12. What platforms or services does the business depend on day to day — payment processors, booking systems, delivery platforms, marketplaces?
13. What would happen if your main platform went down or changed its terms?
14. Are you dependent on any single platform for a significant portion of your revenue?
15. Have you ever been burned by a platform change — algorithm, fees, policy?

**Layer 4 — Formal Partnerships**

16. Do you have any formal partnerships or agreements with other businesses?
17. What does each partnership involve — revenue sharing, referrals, co-delivery, co-marketing?
18. Are the terms of those partnerships written down and agreed on clearly?
19. Are those partnerships actually producing value — or are they inactive?
20. Have you tried any partnerships in the past that didn't work out — what happened?

**Layer 5 — Informal Dependencies**

21. Who do you rely on regularly without any formal agreement?
22. What would happen if that person became unavailable?
23. Is there anyone you're overly dependent on that you've never formalized a relationship with?

**Layer 6 — Professional Relationships**

24. Do you have an accountant or bookkeeper — how involved are they?
25. Do you have a lawyer you can call when needed?
26. Do you have a mentor, advisor, or coach — how often do you talk?
27. Do you have a banker or financial contact who knows your business?
28. Which of these professional relationships is missing that you most need?

**Layer 7 — Risk & Concentration**

29. What is your single biggest dependency — the one relationship or platform that, if lost, would hurt most?
30. Do you have any single supplier, referral source, or partner that makes up more than 30% of your business?
31. What are you doing to reduce that concentration risk?
32. Have you ever lost a key partner or supplier — what did that cost you?

**Layer 8 — Untapped Partnerships**

33. What businesses serve the same customer as you but don't compete with you?
34. Have you explored any of those as potential referral or collaboration partners?
35. Is there a business you could partner with that would immediately give you access to their customer base?
36. What would you offer a potential partner in return?

**Layer 9 — Referral Program**

37. Do you have a formal referral program — what's the incentive?
38. Do your best referral sources know you appreciate them — how do you show it?
39. How do you track where referrals come from?
40. What would make someone refer you more often than they already do?

**Layer 10 — Relationship Management**

41. Which key external relationship are you taking for granted right now?
42. When did you last proactively reach out to a key partner or referral source — not because you needed something?
43. What would it look like to actively invest in your most valuable external relationships?

## Bucket 11 — Competitors

**Layer 1 — Direct Competitors**

1. Who are your direct competitors — businesses selling the same thing to the same customer?
2. Who is the biggest or most successful competitor in your space?
3. How long have they been operating?
4. Are they local, national, or online?
5. What do they charge — how does their pricing compare to yours?
6. How many direct competitors are there — is the market crowded or wide open?

**Layer 2 — Indirect Competitors**

7. Who are your indirect competitors — businesses solving the same problem a different way?
8. What alternatives does your customer have besides you and your direct competitors?
9. What would a customer do if your business didn't exist — where would they go?

**Layer 3 — Market Landscape**

10. Is your market growing, stable, or shrinking?
11. Are competitors making money — or is everyone in this market struggling?
12. Is there a gap in the market nobody is serving — a customer nobody is reaching?
13. Is there space to create your own category — or is everyone fighting over the same customers?
14. How easy is it for new competitors to enter your market?

**Layer 4 — Competitor Strengths**

15. What do your best competitors do really well?
16. What do customers love about them — what keeps people loyal to them?
17. What does your strongest competitor do that you currently can't match?
18. What can you learn from your most successful competitor right now?

**Layer 5 — Competitor Weaknesses**

19. Where do your competitors consistently fail or disappoint customers?
20. What do their unhappy customers complain about most?
21. What gap are they leaving open that you could fill?
22. What do you do better than every competitor in your market?

**Layer 6 — Review Mining**

23. Have you read your competitors' five-star reviews — what do customers love most about them?
24. Have you read their one and two-star reviews — where do they consistently fail?
25. What exact words do customers use to describe competitors — in reviews, comments, social media?
26. What do those reviews tell you about what the market actually wants that nobody is fully delivering?

**Layer 7 — Social Media Benchmarks**

27. Whose Instagram or TikTok do you follow for content inspiration — not necessarily a competitor?
28. What makes their content work — what do they do that you want to replicate?
29. Which account in your industry has the strongest social media presence — what are they doing?
30. Which account outside your industry do you admire most for their content — why?

**Layer 8 — Pricing Benchmarks**

31. Who do you compare your prices against when deciding what to charge?
32. Are you pricing above, below, or the same as the market average?
33. Is your market price-driven — do customers shop primarily on price?
34. Who charges the most in your market — how do they justify it?

**Layer 9 — Customer Experience Benchmarks**

35. Who delivers the best customer experience in your industry — what do they do specifically?
36. Is there a business outside your industry whose customer experience you want to model?
37. Where does your customer experience fall short compared to the best in your market?
38. What would have to change in your business to deliver the best customer experience in your space?

**Layer 10 — Marketing Benchmarks**

39. Whose ads do you see repeatedly and think — that's working well?
40. Whose website or landing page do you think is really effective — what makes it work?
41. Which competitor's marketing do you most respect — what are they doing that you aren't?
42. What marketing channel is a competitor using successfully that you haven't tried yet?

**Layer 11 — Business Model Benchmarks**

43. Is there a business — in any industry — that you want yours to look like one day?
44. What specifically about their model do you want to replicate?
45. Is there a business that started where you are now and got to where you want to be — what did they do?

**Layer 12 — Switching**

46. What makes customers leave a competitor and come to you — what do they say?
47. What makes customers leave you and go to a competitor — what do you think the real reason is?
48. Have you ever won a customer back from a competitor — how?

**Layer 13 — Positioning in Customer's Mind**

49. If a customer was comparing you to your top competitor — what would they say is the difference?
50. What do you think customers believe about your business vs your strongest competitor?
51. Is there a gap between how you want to be perceived and how you're actually perceived?

**Layer 14 — Learning from Competitors**

52. What is your strongest competitor teaching you right now — for free?
53. What would you do differently if you studied your top three competitors seriously for one week?
54. What is one thing a competitor is doing that you could implement in your business this month?

## Bucket 12 — Tools & Systems

**Layer 1 — Payment Tools**

1. How do you take payments — Square, Stripe, PayPal, bank transfer, cash, other?
2. Do you invoice customers — what do you use?
3. Do you have any recurring or subscription payments set up — how are those managed?
4. Have you had any payment problems — chargebacks, late payments, cash collection issues?

5. What do you use for accounting and bookkeeping — QuickBooks, Wave, spreadsheet, nothing?
6. Do you use separate software for invoicing, expenses, and taxes — or one tool for everything?
7. Who does your taxes — you, an accountant, a service?
8. How often do you look at your financial data in these tools?

9. How do you track your customers and leads — a CRM, a spreadsheet, WhatsApp, your memory?

12. Have you ever lost a lead or a customer because you forgot to follow up?

**Layer 8 — Ads & Promotion**

**Layer 9 — Content Creation**

61. Which tools are you paying for that you barely use?
62. Which tool gives you the most value relative to what it costs?

**Layer 16 — Scalability**

66. What tool or system would you need to add before you could scale?

## Bucket 13 — Goals & Vision

**Layer 1 — Short Term Goals**

1. What do you want this business to look like in 12 months — specifically, not generally?
2. What are the three most important things that must happen in the next 90 days?
3. What would have to be true at the end of this year for you to feel good about it?
4. What is the one number — revenue, profit, customers, whatever matters most to you — you want to hit this year?

**Layer 2 — Medium Term Vision**

5. What does this business look like in 3 years — describe it specifically?
6. How many people work in it, what do you sell, who are your customers, where do you operate?
7. What are you personally doing in the business in 3 years — same as today or different?
8. What needs to be built or changed between now and then to get there?

**Layer 3 — Long Term Target**

9. What is the big long term destination — where do you want this to be in 10 years?
10. Is there a size, a reputation, a market position you're aiming for?
11. What would make you look back in 10 years and feel like this was worth it?

**Layer 4 — Exit Strategy**

12. Are you building this to keep forever, to sell one day, or to pass on to family?
13. If you were going to sell — when, to whom, and for how much?
14. Is there a version of this business you'd be proud to hand to someone else — what does it look like?
15. Have you thought about what happens to the business if something happens to you?

**Layer 5 — Financial Definition of Success**

16. What does financial success look like to you — a specific monthly number, a lifestyle, a net worth?
17. What is the minimum the business needs to generate for you to feel it's working?
18. What number would make you feel completely free — financially secure, no longer worried?
19. Are you building for income now or wealth over time — or both?

**Layer 6 — Core Values**

20. What does this business stand for — what principles would you never compromise on?
21. How do you want customers to feel every time they interact with your business?
22. What behavior would get someone fired from your business immediately?
23. If your business had a personality — how would you describe it?

**Layer 7 — Core Focus**

24. What is the one thing your business does better than anyone else?
25. If you had to cut everything except one product, one service, one customer — what would you keep?
26. What is the one thing you want to be known for in your market?

**Layer 8 — Why**

27. Why does this business exist beyond making money?
28. What problem in the world does it solve — who is better off because it exists?
29. What would be lost if your business disappeared tomorrow?
30. What do you believe about your customers or your industry that drives everything you do?

**Layer 9 — Personal Vision**

31. What kind of life are you building — not just what kind of business?
32. What does a perfect week look like for you — personally and professionally?
33. What do you want more of in your life that this business should eventually give you?
34. What do you want less of — what are you trying to escape?
35. Is the business you're building actually aligned with the life you want?

**Layer 10 — Hedgehog Concept**

36. What are you deeply passionate about in this business — what energizes you most?
37. What could you be the best at in your market — genuinely better than anyone?
38. What drives your economic engine — what single metric, if improved, would most impact your financial results?
39. Where do those three things overlap — that intersection is where you should focus everything?

**Layer 11 — Brand Promise**

40. What do you guarantee every customer every time — not in legal terms, in human terms?
41. What should a customer always be able to count on when they choose you?
42. Has your business ever broken that promise — what happened?

**Layer 12 — Long Game**

43. Are you making decisions for this year or for the next decade?
44. What are you doing right now for short-term revenue that might be hurting you long-term?
45. What would you do differently if you knew you'd be running this business for 20 more years?
46. What are you avoiding that you know you'll have to face eventually?

**Layer 13 — What's Been Tried**

47. What have you already tried toward your vision that didn't work — what happened?
48. What have you invested in — time, money, energy — that didn't move you forward?
49. What lesson from a past failure is most relevant to where you're trying to go now?

**Layer 14 — Fear & Resistance**

50. What are you afraid of when you think about where you want to take this business?
51. What's the worst case scenario you're trying to avoid — is that fear driving your decisions?
52. What would you do differently if you weren't afraid of that outcome?

**Layer 15 — Legacy**

53. What do you want to have built by the end — what's the legacy?
54. Who do you want to have impacted — customers, community, family, industry?
55. What do you want people to say about this business after you're gone?
56. Is there something bigger than profit that this business is working toward?

**Layer 16 — Alignment**

57. Are your daily decisions aligned with your long term vision — or pulling against it?
58. What is the biggest gap between where you are today and where you want to be?
59. What is the one thing you keep saying you'll do later that you need to start now?
60. If nothing changes in the next 12 months — where does that leave you?
